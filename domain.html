01Research context

Research Background

Introduction

The rapid growth of digital service platforms has transformed how customers discover professionals, compare services and evaluate providers through ratings and feedback. Yet identifying a suitable and trustworthy provider remains difficult. Traditional platforms commonly rely on keywords, predefined categories, average ratings and basic filters that may not capture an individual customer's requirements, context or trust expectations.

Customers frequently describe a service problem, expected completion time, preferred location and other requirements in a single natural-language request. A recommendation system must understand both the meaning of that request and the characteristics of available providers. Selection also depends on availability, contextual suitability, previous experiences, review reliability and service quality—not skills alone.

Motivation

Beyond category matching

A category match may still be unavailable, contextually unsuitable or supported by unreliable feedback. A high average rating alone does not fully represent service quality.

Proposed research

An integrated framework

Weda.lk combines lexical and semantic matching, credibility-aware personalization, contextual refinement, review credibility, aspect-level quality and explainable ranking.

Expected significance

  • More relevant service-provider recommendations.
  • Improved understanding of natural-language service requests.
  • Context-sensitive matching and better consideration of review credibility.
  • Transparent, explainable provider rankings.
  • An integrated framework for service-provider recommendation research.

These are intended research contributions, not claims of experimentally verified improvement.

02Related work

Literature Survey

The research draws upon content-based filtering, semantic text representation, collaborative filtering, context-aware recommendation, review credibility analysis and explainable AI. Each addresses a different aspect of service-provider matching.

Content-based recommendation

TF-IDF represents word importance and supports lexical comparison between requests and provider descriptions, but may struggle when similar concepts use different words.

Semantic recommendation

Multilingual sentence embeddings represent meaning as vectors, complementing lexical matching when wording or language varies.

Collaborative filtering

Historical ratings, selections and interaction patterns support personalization, although interaction frequency alone does not establish provider reliability.

Context-aware recommendation

Availability, location, timing and service-specific constraints help determine whether an initially relevant provider suits the current request.

Review credibility

Isolation Forest can estimate anomalous review patterns. An anomaly is an indicator for credibility processing—not proof of fraud.

Aspect evaluation & XAI

Quality, punctuality, communication and price reveal more than a single rating, while explanations communicate why a provider ranks highly or poorly.

04Core inquiry

Research Problem

How can an integrated intelligent recommendation framework combine hybrid relevance matching, temporal and contextual refinement, and trust-aware review intelligence to identify suitable and reliable service providers while generating understandable recommendation explanations?

Natural-language intent may exceed keyword matching; a relevant provider may fail contextual requirements; unreliable reviews may distort ranking; and a single average rating cannot explain performance across quality dimensions. These linked challenges require progressive evaluation instead of a single ranking signal.

Research questions

  1. RQ1How can lexical relevance, multilingual semantic similarity and credibility-aware collaborative filtering improve natural-language matching?
  2. RQ2How can temporal and contextual information refine an initial provider set?
  3. RQ3How can skills and credibility be investigated through an independent verification-focused component?
  4. RQ4How can review credibility, weighted aspects and XAI produce trustworthy final rankings?
  5. RQ5How can the integrated framework be evaluated for relevance, ranking quality, reliability and interpretability?

Research questions remain subject to comparison with the officially approved proposal.

Component 3 · Skill Verification and Credibility

Investigates provider skills and credibility independently. Its verified methodology, input data and evaluation approach must come from the Component 3 documentation. It does not filter candidates between C2 and C4.

C1

Hybrid Recommendation Methodology

  1. Prepare natural-language requests and provider descriptions.
  2. Calculate TF-IDF lexical similarity.
  3. Calculate multilingual sentence-embedding semantic similarity.
  4. Construct credibility-aware collaborative signals from normalized ratings, booking success and transformed interaction frequency; optionally apply a controlled loyalty boost.
  5. Normalize and fuse signals conceptually: Hybrid Score = w1 × Lexical + w2 × Semantic + w3 × Collaborative.
  6. Rank and return the top 20 relevant providers.

Actual weights and normalization details require verification from C1 implementation.

C2

Temporal and Contextual Matching

Receives 20 candidates and refines them using verified availability, timing, geographic and service-context features.

Output: 10 contextually suitable providers.

Exact features, algorithm and scoring rules must come from C2 documentation.

C4

Trust-Aware Review Intelligence

  1. Prepare review data for credibility and aspect analysis.
  2. Use Isolation Forest to estimate anomalous review patterns and credibility scores.
  3. Evaluate credible reviews across Quality, Time/Punctuality, Communication and Price.
  4. Fuse normalized aspects conceptually: Final Aspect Score = wQ × Quality + wT × Time + wC × Communication + wP × Price.
  5. Convert aspect scores into understandable explanations.
  6. Rank candidates and return the explainable top five.

The documented examples mention credibility 0.5 and explanation thresholds 0.75/0.45; these remain verification items, not final published parameters.
